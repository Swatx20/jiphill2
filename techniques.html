The Dolly Zoom

A camera technique that creates a disorienting perspective shift.

The Dolly Zoom — also known as the "Vertigo Effect" — is achieved by simultaneously moving the camera toward or away from the subject while adjusting the zoom lens in the opposite direction. This keeps the subject at a constant size in the frame while the background appears to expand or compress, producing a disorienting perspective shift that externalizes a character's inner psychological conflict.

The Long Take

Continuous, unbroken shots that pull the audience into real time.

The Long Take is a continuous shot held for an extended duration without cuts. By refusing to break the footage, directors create immersive realism — the audience shares the real-time experience of the character, heightening tension, empathy, and spatial awareness.

Slow Motion (Overcranking)

High frame rates that lend weight to a single moment.

Slow Motion, or overcranking, is achieved by shooting at frame rates higher than the standard 24 fps so that playback appears slowed. Overcranking strips away the blur of ordinary motion and forces the audience to dwell on a moment, lending emotional gravity to actions that would otherwise pass in an instant.
