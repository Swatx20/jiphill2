Establishing Perspective

By utilizing the vastness of these angles, we don't just see a location; we experience the scale of the world. These shots serve as the visual anchor, providing the necessary context to understand the scope of the journey before the camera captures the intimate details of the narrative.

Wide Angle

Creates a sense of space and context, allowing the audience to understand the environment and the relationship between characters and their surroundings.

Full Shot

Emphasizes the scale of the character within the environment.

Character Dynamics

The geometry of a shot dictates the emotional weight of a character. Whether we are looking up to establish authority or tilting the frame to signal psychological chaos, these angles act as a silent narrator telling the audience exactly how to feel about the balance of power in any given scene.

Low Angle

Establishes the dominance or power of a character by looking up at them.

High Angle

Diminishes the power of a character by looking down on them.

Dutch Angle

Conveys psychological uneasiness and tension.

Close Up

Completes the emotional impact by focusing on a character's expressions.
